01 — the big idea

What even is a distribution?

Before KL Loss, let's get comfortable with distributions. A probability distribution tells you how likely each outcome is. Think of it as a "belief" about what will happen.

Below are two distributions — P (the true one) and Q (our guess). Drag the sliders and watch them shift.

P peak position 35%
Q peak position 65%

When P and Q overlap perfectly, they're identical. When they drift apart — that gap is exactly what KL divergence measures.

02 — the formula

What KL divergence actually measures

KL divergence answers this question: "How much extra information do I waste if I use Q to describe a world that's actually P?"

KL(P ∥ Q) = Σ P(x) · log( P(x) / Q(x) )
Sum over all outcomes x. P is truth, Q is our approximation.

Three things to notice:

① It's not symmetric — KL(P∥Q) ≠ KL(Q∥P). Direction matters.
② It's always ≥ 0. Zero means the distributions are identical.
③ The log ratio captures surprise — when Q says something is rare but P says it's common, that's expensive.

04 — in the wild: LLMs

Where does this show up in AI?

When training a language model with PPO (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback), we want the new model to improve — but not drift too far from the original. KL divergence is the leash.

📦 Parent model
Original pretrained LLM
Distribution = P
→ fine-tune →
🎯 Student model
Fine-tuned version
Distribution = Q

PPO loss = reward signal − β · KL(Q ∥ P)
The KL term punishes the student for straying too far from the parent.

06 — forward vs reverse

KL(P∥Q) vs KL(Q∥P) — does direction matter?

Yes — a lot. They measure different things and lead to different model behaviors.

Forward KL(P ∥ Q)

Penalizes Q for putting low probability where P puts high probability. Q tries to cover all modes of P — even if it spreads too thin.

Reverse KL(Q ∥ P)

Penalizes Q for putting high probability where P puts low probability. Q picks one mode and sharpens onto it — mode-seeking behavior.

The On-Policy Self-Distillation method uses reverse KL. The student model generates outputs, then is trained to match its own good generations — making it sharper and more confident rather than spreading probability everywhere.

The 30-second summary

🔵 KL divergence measures how different distribution Q is from distribution P.
📐 KL(P∥Q) = Σ P(x) · log(P/Q) — always non-negative, zero when identical.
🤖 In LLMs (PPO), it keeps the student model from drifting too far from the parent.
🔄 Reverse KL is used in self-distillation for sharper, mode-seeking behavior.
